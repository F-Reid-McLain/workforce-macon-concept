About this map and story

Net commuting = jobs located in the county minus employed residents. Commuting and pay: LODES 2023. Population: ACS 2024. In the story, timing is illustrative and heights exaggerate the change 2.5×. Click the Highways button for the region's major roads.

Presenting this story? Press P or ▶ Present for full screen.

  • Real data. Commuting comes from the U.S. Census Bureau's LODES 2023, which counts jobs, not people: someone with two jobs counts twice. Residents come from the American Community Survey 2024.
  • From everywhere. Dots between counties are real flows between Macon-Bibb and each neighbor. Dots to and from the edge of the map stand for everyone else: all of Macon-Bibb's commuting with places beyond its neighbors, and each neighbor's net exchange with places other than Macon-Bibb. Together, each county's midday change equals its net commuting on the map.
  • Farther is fuzzier. LODES sometimes places a job at a company's reporting address rather than where the work happens, which mostly inflates long-distance flows. That's why those dots are fainter.
  • Illustrative. Departure and return times, straight-line routes, and every job filled at once. Real days include shift work, part-time jobs and people working from home.
  • Drawn to be seen. Heights exaggerate each county's change 2.5×, and each dot stands for about jobs. The labels and the counter show the real figures.

Jobs · What's growing

Manufacturing is growing three times as fast as health care in Macon.

Macon added 1,100 factory jobs in the past five years, paying $65,800 on average, about $10,000 above the $55,400 average across all industries here. And 640 more were announced in 2026 alone.

    Registered nurses are Macon's most common job: 2,779 positions averaging $87,600.
